Who is at the end of the canal

Water that leaks out on the way never reaches them.

500 acres ploughed under, and a 40-year job gone

Mike England farms near Mercedes. In 2024, with no irrigation water, he destroyed 500 acres of sugar cane and laid off his workers.

“One of them has worked for me for 40-some-odd years. We were just past teenagers when he started here. You think I liked laying him off?”

That February the last sugar mill in Texas closed after 51 years. More than 500 people worked there.

Texas Tribune, April 2024 · Texas Standard, February 2024

A town came dangerously close to running out of drinking water

In August 2022 Falcon Lake fell to 9% full. Zapata's intake was pulling up mud and its pumps were failing. Downstream in Edcouch, the city engineer warned residents:

“There's basically no water, and we could expect shutdowns from a day up to three days.”

In the Valley, city water rides to the treatment plant on top of farm water, in the same canals. When farms get none, a city has to spend its own water just to push the rest through.

Texas Tribune, October 2022 · Texas Public Radio, August 2022 · Texas Tribune, July 2024

The first year in 38 without irrigation water

Brian Jones is a fourth-generation Valley farmer. In 2024 he planted half his farm, let some employees go and cut the hours of the rest.

“Without water, what are we using to grow our crops? What are we able to pay back those loans with?”

Texas Tribune, April 2024

Sealing a canal cannot make it rain. It can make sure the water that is released reaches a field or a tap, instead of the bed of a ditch. RioIQ exists to get the most out of every gallon Texas has left.
